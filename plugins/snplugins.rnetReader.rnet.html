snplugins.rnetReader.rnet module¶

Read a Rep Plus net file (“.rnet”: the nets of Brian Gaines’s CNet and KNet, in Rep Plus 2 and its forerunners) into plain Python objects (see parse()), with nothing of Semantic Nets in it: reader makes the model and view from what this finds.

The file is text: UTF-8 (with a byte order mark), its lines ended by CRs (or LFs), its fields by tabs. The first line is a header, \tC\t<version>\t<background colour>..., and the rest are sections, each a line naming it and how many lines it has, and then those lines:

D settings: a name and a value on each line (eg: “Date”, “NodeType”).

(Versions 6 and 7 have a “PS:” line instead: “PS:CNettT:Allt…”.)

T the node types: shape, a variant of it, font, size, bold, flags, four

colours (see NodeType), …, and the name, last.

A the link types: in versions 7 and 8, and some 9s, four fields –

bits (an arrowhead at the destination, at the source, a bar, …), two colours and the name; in version 9, eleven – the head’s shape and bars, the tail’s shape and bars, the line’s flags, a symbol in the middle, four colours, and the name (see LinkType). Version 6 has none: its links’ codes are the bits themselves.

M the kinds of mark (see MarkKind): two colours and the name. N the nodes: type, marks (none in version 6), x, y, width, height,

label, and (from version 7) one more field. A node whose type is prefixed by “P” or “G” is a picture or a drawing: a drawing’s records (rectangles, lines, text…) follow it, in lines of their own.

L the links: source and destination nodes (by their place in N,

from 0), the link types (by their place in A): the link’s from the source to the destination, and, if there’s a second (after a space), the type of a link back, from the destination to the source – Rep Plus’s Link.Type(0) and Type(1), drawn side by side – and, in some files, more fields (its flags, and a label: eg, a weight).

G the groups (panels: parts of the net that go together): the nodes in

each, and in a second field (if any) the groups in it.

A type’s colours, and a link type’s, are 6 hexadecimal digits (RRGGBB), but older files leave out leading zeros (“8A00” for 008A00). A label’s line breaks are U+FFFD characters (as CNet wrote them).

Worked out from the files themselves (and the SVG pictures CNet exported of some of them): see NodeType and LinkType for what’s known, and guessed, of what their fields mean.

class snplugins.rnetReader.rnet.NodeType(index, name, shape, variant, font, size, bold, flags, colors, extra='', fields=<factory>)¶

Bases: object

A node type (a line of section T).

CNet’s SVG pictures of its nets show that of the four colours, the second is the fill and the third the text’s (eg: “All”: 008A00, D8FFFF, 006400, 008A00 is drawn as an ellipse filled with D8FFFF, outlined in 008A00, with text in 006400). The outline is the first or the fourth: they’re the same in every type those pictures show.

class snplugins.rnetReader.rnet.LinkType(index, name, head=0, headBars=0, tail=0, tailBars=0, lineFlags=1, mid=0, colors=<factory>, bits=None, fields=<factory>)¶

Bases: object

A link type (a line of section A): how its links are drawn, and its name.

Versions 7 and 8 (and some files of version 9) give it as bits (see HEAD, TAIL, BAR, HEAVY and DOUBLE) and two colours, the line’s and the arrowheads’ and bars’ (as CNet’s SVG pictures show). Version 9 gives the head’s shape (head: 0 none, 1 an arrowhead, 2 a heavy one, 3 a double one) and bars (headBars: 0 to 3), the same at the tail, the line’s flags (4: drawn dashed: see dashed), a symbol in the middle (mid), and four colours. For version 6, which has no link types, they’re made from the codes its links have, which are the bits.
