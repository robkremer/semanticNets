snplugins.rnetReader.reader module¶

Make a Semantic Nets model of a Rep Plus net (see rnet), for view to show (see buildModel()), built on ONTOLOGY (the kernel of Gaines’s protologic, so that the reasoners draw KNet’s inferences):

  • Each node type (section T) becomes a node type (an SN subtype of KNODE: a KNet node, with its truth marks’ bars and menu items – see hooks) of the same name, with its shape and colours (see NODE_SHAPES and NODE_COLORS); one that’s just its text (eg: “Title”, “Note”) has no outline or fill. A node with no type gets UNTYPED. One with the enclosure shape (ENCLOSURE: eg, “construct”, “frame”, “rules”) becomes an SN subtype of context instead, that doesn’t quote what it contains (see GraphObject.quotesContents()): its nodes are boxes of facts – but one of no size (an invisible end of links CNet draws, eg: in a drawing) is a POINT, with its type’s name in its “rnetType” attribute.

  • Each link type (section A) becomes a relation type of the same name, drawn with its arrowheads, bars and middle symbol as relation marks (see endMarks() and MID_MARKS), in its colour (see LINK_COLORS), as a straight line, with no label: a dashed one’s colours are faded. It’s an SN subtype of the kernel relation type its look says it is (see kernelKind(): eg, an arrow is includes, an arrow with a bar excludes), if any – or of the connective’s, if it has a heavy or a double arrowhead (see connectiveKind(): eg, “Conjunct includes”), whose links define a conjunction or a disjunction (Gaines’s structural connectives) – or of PREFER, if it’s a plain line with a “>” in the middle (see preferenceKind(): a preference among a connective’s cases, for its defaults). One drawn backwards – its arrow at its tail (eg: “Includes reverse”) – is drawn the other way round instead: its arrow at its destination, and its links from the file’s destination to its source (see isReversed()), so that it means what it shows.

  • Each node (section N) becomes an individual of its type, named by its label (its line breaks and all; one with no label has no name). Its mark, if it has one, becomes attributes – “mark” (T, F or TF: true, false, or both), “markKind” (eg: “Asserted”), and “markDerived” (if it was inferred, rather than asserted) – with “marks”, the number as the file has it (in hexadecimal), and its label says the mark (see LABEL). It’s drawn as Rep Plus draws it (see rnet.NetNode): filled with the mark kind’s background colour, and its outline in the kind’s colour, if the mark says so. An asserted mark is the node’s truth mark, too, which the reasoners propagate: hooks.MARK_TRUE and/or hooks.MARK_FALSE, saying its kind (eg: “asserted”), shown as Gaines’s bars. (A derived one isn’t: it’s what the reasoners conclude again.) Pictures and drawings aren’t made (they aren’t concepts).

  • Each link (section L) becomes a relation of its type from its source to its destination – and one of its second type, if it has one, back from its destination to its source (see rnet.Link) – or the other way, for a reversed type (as it’s drawn). Any more fields it has (eg: a weight) are in its “values” attribute. Derived links (CNet’s or KNet’s inferences: see rnet.LinkType.derived) are made as they are: they’re what the reasoners infer anyway.

  • Each box (a node of an enclosure type) contains – by “contains” relations – the nodes whose centres are inside it, and the smaller boxes that are: each in the smallest box it’s in (CNet lists them nowhere: it’s where they are).

  • A construct box’s poles (the nodes it contains) exclude each other, as CNet’s construct macro says, unless an includes link (or a chain of them) joins them: each pair gets a relation of CONSTRUCT_EXCLUDES, an SN subtype of the net’s own symmetric exclusion, faded as derived links are (see addConstructMeaning()).

  • Nodes with the same label are one node, as KNet takes them (a net can draw a node again, elsewhere, to keep its links short): each copy after the first gets a relation of SAME_LABEL, an equivalence, to the first (see addSameLabels()) – unless buildModel() is told not to (a figure of several copies of one net, side by side).

  • Groups (section G) aren’t made: in Rep Plus they’re only a way to select and drag nodes together, with no meaning (and they’d fight the layouts).

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.ONTOLOGY = '/Users/kremer/git/semanticNets/plugins/rnetReader/knet.snm'¶

The model the models made build on: the kernel of Gaines’s protologic – its relation types (KERNEL) and inference patterns, as rules (made by tools/makeKnetRules.py, whose doc says what they are).

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.KERNEL = ('includes', 'equivalent', 'excludes', 'coexcludes', 'negates', 'inconsistent')¶

The kernel relation types’ names, in ONTOLOGY.

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.CONNECTIVES = {(2, 0): 'conjunct includes', (2, 1): 'conjunct excludes', (3, 0): 'disjunct includes', (3, 2): 'disjunct coexcludes'}¶

The structural connectives’ relation types, in ONTOLOGY (SN subtypes of the kernel’s: see tools/makeKnetRules.py), by the arrowhead (a heavy one, a conjunction’s; a double one, a disjunction’s: rnet.LinkType.head) and its bars: see connectiveKind().

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.KNODE = 'knode'¶

The SN type of a KNet net’s nodes, in ONTOLOGY: the net’s node types are its SN subtypes.

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.ARROWHEADS = (1, 2, 3)¶

The ends (rnet.LinkType.head, tail) that are arrowheads: plain, heavy (a conjunction’s) and double (a disjunction’s) – each an ordinary link, as far as the kernel goes (Gaines: their connective’s iterative clause comes on top).

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.BY_BARS = {0: 'includes', 1: 'excludes', 2: 'coexcludes', 3: 'negates'}¶

An arrowhead’s bars (or a line’s middle symbol: rnet.LinkType.mid) -> the kernel relation type it is.

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.NOT_KERNEL = ('generic', 'etc', 'prefer', 'preferred', 'prohibited', 'forbidden', 'defeasible', 'conjecture', 'possible', 'particular')¶

Words in a link type’s name that say it isn’t the kernel link it looks like: generic links stand for any of them (Gaines, section 2.7); “Etc” links for more of the same, in pictures; preferences and defaults are defeasible; the rest are prohibitions and modalities.

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.NODE_SHAPES = {0: 'Rectangle', 1: 'Rectangle', 2: 'Oval', 3: 'Rectangle'}¶

A node type’s shape (rnet.NodeType.shape) -> its shape here (“vis/shape”). A grid’s (“rgrid”) is a FileFolder. An enclosure’s (see ENCLOSURE) is a box, drawn as a window.

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.NODE_COLORS = {'vis/borderColor': 3, 'vis/fillColor': 1, 'vis/textColor': 2}¶

Where a node type’s colours go (which of its four, from 0): the second is the fill and the third the text’s (as CNet’s SVG pictures of its nets show); the fourth – or the first: they’re the same in every type in those pictures – the outline.

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.LINK_COLORS = {'vis/lineColor': 3}¶

Where a (version 9) link type’s four colours go: the line’s (and its marks’) is taken to be the fourth, as a node’s outline is (eg: “Prefer”, whose last two are purple, was purple in the older files’ link types). A link type given by its bits (see rnet.LinkType) has two: the line’s, and its marks’.

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.ARROW = 'arrow'¶

An arrowhead (pointing off the end it’s at).

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.HEAVY_ARROW = 'arrow(11,13,6)'¶

A heavy arrowhead (a conjunction’s).

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.MID_MARKS = {1: '|', 2: '||', 3: '|||', 4: '@', 6: '>'}¶

A link type’s symbol in the middle (rnet.LinkType.mid) -> its middle marks: the symmetric exclusion, coexclusion and negation (as one, two and three bars, as their arrows have at the head), prohibited, preferred.

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.DERIVED_FADE = 0.5¶

How far a derived link type’s colour is faded toward white (CNet draws them dashed).

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.MARK_WIDTH = 2¶

The width of the outline a mark draws (in its kind’s colour) on a node whose type has none (as Rep Plus does).

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.LABEL = '$name${mark:+ $mark}'¶

Each node type’s label template: the name, and the mark, if there is one (see the module doc).

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.UNTYPED = 'text'¶

The node type made for nodes with none (eg: text the program wrote): just its text.

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.POINT = 'point'¶

The node type made for nodes of an enclosure type that have no size: invisible ends of links (eg: in a drawing).

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.ENCLOSURE = 3¶

A node type’s shape (rnet.NodeType.shape) that’s an enclosure: a box that holds the nodes inside it (RepNet manual, sections 3.7 and 6.2). Every node type of the test nets named for a box (“collection”, “frame”, “context”, “construct”, “cases”, “rules”, “Context:”, “Cluster”) has it.

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.TITLEBAR_TINT = 0.8¶

How far a box’s title bar is faded from its outline colour toward white.

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.CONSTRUCT = 'construct'¶

The name (any case; it may go on: eg, “Construct:”) of the enclosure type whose boxes are Kelly constructs.

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.CONSTRUCT_EXCLUDES = 'construct excludes'¶

The relation type of the exclusions a construct box’s poles get (see addConstructMeaning()).

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.PREFER = 'prefer'¶

The preferences’ relation type, in ONTOLOGY: links of a line with the preference symbol in its middle (rnet.LinkType.mid 6), and no arrowheads, are SN subtypes of it (see preferenceKind()).

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.SAME_LABEL = 'same label'¶

The relation type of the equivalences between nodes with the same label (see addSameLabels()).

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.KNET_NODE_TYPES = ('concept', 'context', 'disjoint', 'construct', 'cases', 'rules', 'collection')¶

The node types (their names, in any case) of the nodes KNet reasons about: it leaves out the others (eg: titles, notes, grids, frames).

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.KNET_NODE = 'knetNode'¶

The attribute of a node type (an SN subtype of KNODE) that says whether its nodes are the net’s: False for one not in KNET_NODE_TYPES (eg: a note’s), whose nodes KNet doesn’t reason about – so they aren’t probed (see probes.netNodes()).

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.kernelKind(t)¶
Parameters:

t (LinkType)

Returns:

The kernel relation type (KERNEL) that links of t are, as its look says (Gaines’s notation): an arrow is includes, and one with one, two or three bars at its head excludes, coexcludes or negates (a heavy or a double arrowhead – a conjunction’s or a disjunction’s – is still an arrow); a line with one, two or three bars across its middle (and no arrowheads, or one at each end) is the symmetric exclusion, coexclusion or negation; an arrow at each end is equivalent, and so is a plain line named so. None for anything else – generic links, preferences, prohibitions, lines – and for a type whose name says it isn’t (see NOT_KERNEL).

Return type:

str | None

None for anything else.

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.addSameLabels(b)¶

Make the nodes with the same label (see sameLabel()) equivalent: KNet takes them to be one node (eg: a net that shows a node in two places, to keep its links short). Each after the first gets a relation of SAME_LABEL (an SN subtype of the kernel’s equivalent, faded as derived links are, and noted as added by the reader) to the first. Just the nodes KNet reasons about (see KNET_NODE_TYPES), and not those with no label. (KNet’s one node is a little more than equivalent ones: the defining links of each are all one definition – see sn.model.reasoner.DEFINING.)

snplugins.rnetReader.reader.addConstructMeaning(b)¶

Add what CNet’s construct macro means (RepNet manual, section 3.7): the poles of a construct box (the nodes it holds: see enclosed()) are linked by mutual opposition, unless that conflicts with an ordination link – an includes link (see _isIncludes()), or a chain of them, from one pole to the other. Each pair that’s opposed, and isn’t linked so already, gets a relation of CONSTRUCT_EXCLUDES (an SN subtype of the net’s symmetric exclusion, or of a new one if it has none), drawn faded as CNet’s derived links are, and noted as added by the reader – so it can be hidden, or taken out, by its type.
