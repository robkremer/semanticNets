sn.view.graphics.balloonLayout module¶

Round layouts (“arrange now”, and “keep arranged”): a view’s nodes arranged in circles.

  • BalloonLayout (“balloon”): trees, by a relation type, as balloons: each node’s children in a ring around it, each child’s subtree a balloon of its own, fanned out away from its parent.

  • RadialLayout (“radial”): trees, by a relation type, in rings: each root in the middle, each depth on a circle around it, each subtree in a wedge as wide as it needs.

  • HubLayout (“hubs”): by every relation shown: the nodes with the most relations are the hubs, each with the nodes nearest it (its spokes) in a balloon around it, and the hubs of a connected group on a circle, their spokes fanned out away from its middle (where the relations between the hubs go).

The geometry, on its own (no view): balloonPositions(), radialPositions(), hubForest() and hubPositions(). Each node’s taken to be a circle, as wide as its box’s diagonal (so nothing overlaps, however it’s turned). A node’s children’s subtrees go round it each in its own wedge: the angle its nodes’ circles take up, seen from the node – so none overlap, nor do the relations from parents to children cross.

Kept arranged (see _RoundLayout), each group’s laid out again after each command, where it is (groupOffset()), and moved off any other (separateGroups()).

sn.view.graphics.balloonLayout.radialPositions(roots, children, sizes, *, spacing=20.0, edgeRoom=None)¶

Lay out some trees radially, each on its own: its root in the middle, each depth on a circle round it, and each node’s subtree in a wedge just as wide as it needs – the most, at any depth, that its nodes there take up (their circles, spacing apart), seen from the middle – each node in the middle of its own, its children’s wedges side by side (in order, clockwise) and centred on it, sharing out what’s spare in its wedge – but over no more than RADIAL_SPREAD: so each subtree stays close to the line out from the middle through its root, however big it is, rather than being spread round all the room its parent has. (The root’s children go all round, from the top.) Each circle’s as small as it can be: far enough out from the one before for the nodes on both (and edgeRoom), and for each node’s children to fit in front of it (in the angle, seen from the middle, between the tangents to its circle where they meet the next) – and all of them further out, in proportion, if the wedges wouldn’t otherwise fit round.

  • sizes (Mapping[K, tuple[float, float]]) – Each node’s (width, height).

  • spacing (float) – The least space between neighbouring nodes.

  • edgeRoom (Mapping[K, float] | None) – The space between a child (its circle) and its parent’s, if not spacing: eg: for the label of the relation to it.

Where each node’s centre goes, each tree’s root at (0, 0); and each tree: its root, its nodes, and the radius of a circle round it.

sn.view.graphics.balloonLayout.hubForest(nodes, neighbours, minSpokes=3, pinned=())¶

Find the hubs of a graph, and their spokes: in each connected group of nodes, the hubs are those with at least minSpokes neighbours (or, if none has, the one with the most), the most first – but only the core of them, those linked (through their spokes) to two others or more, once those linked to fewer are taken off, again and again (if none’s left – eg: the hubs make a tree – the one with the most neighbours, alone): one that hangs off another goes out beyond it, as its spokes do, so the ring is no bigger than the core needs. (Not a pinned hub: it’s cut out, below.) Every other node is a spoke of the hub nearest it (the first of those as near), under the node next nearer it. The hubs are in order round a ring: each after the one most related to the one before (the relations between their spokes too). A pinned node (eg: one the user’s put somewhere) that’s a spoke, or one of a ring’s hubs, is cut out of its group (with the spokes under it) as the only hub of a group of its own.

Parameters:

  • nodes (Sequence[K]) – The nodes, in order.

  • neighbours (Mapping[K, Sequence[K]]) – What each is related to (both ways), in order.

  • minSpokes (int) – The least neighbours a hub has.

  • pinned (Collection[K]) – The nodes cut out of their groups.

Returns:

Each connected group of more than one node (in the order of nodes; one cut out right after the group it’s cut from): its hubs, in order round its ring, and each node’s children (the hubs the roots); and the nodes related to nothing.

Return type:

tuple[list[tuple[list[K], dict[K, list[K]]]], list[K]]

class sn.view.graphics.balloonLayout._RoundLayout(view, **kwargs)¶

Bases: LayoutHeuristic

What the round layouts share: putting their groups (trees, or hubs’ groups) in rows, the nodes they don’t arrange in rows after them (see OTHERS, HUB_OTHERS), and the labels of the relations from parents to children between them.

As a dynamic layout (see adjust()): after each command, each group is laid out again where it is – its root (or its hubs, on average) where it is (see groupOffset()) – and, if it would be on one before it (from the top), moved to the nearest clear place (see separateGroups()). A node the user drags (and drops) that isn’t a group’s root (or its only hub) is pinned there, as the root (only hub) of a group of its own, with what’s under it, until the next static round layout; dropping a root moves its group. The nodes in no group are put as by the static layout, each time – except those the user drops somewhere, which are pinned there.

static _join(roots, kids, pinned, related=None)¶

Join the trees related to one another (see TREES): a tree whose root is related (see related) to a node in another tree becomes one of that node’s children (the last) – so it’s laid out out beyond it, along the way out from the middle, rather than wherever it was. From the biggest top tree (one whose root isn’t related to another tree), each tree related to one joined so far is joined to it (to the first node it’s related to, in order of the relations made), and so on; then from the biggest of the rest (tops first). class sn.view.graphics.balloonLayout.HubLayout(view, **kwargs)¶

Bases: _RoundLayout

Arrange nodes round hubs (see hubForest() and hubPositions()), by every relation shown – or those its relation table doesn’t ignore (follow: see HUB_RELATIONS): in each connected group, the nodes with at least minSpokes relations (to different nodes) are hubs, each with its spokes (the nodes nearest it) in a balloon round it, and the hubs on a circle, their spokes spread round away from its middle – where the relations between them go. The groups are put one after another in rows, from the top left of what’s arranged; the nodes related to nothing in rows after them, or left where they are (others). The label of the relation from each spoke’s hub (or the spoke it’s under) goes on its line, between its ends; the others’ are left between their ends. Kept arranged: see _RoundLayout (a node dropped is cut out of its group: see hubForest()).

Relation types shown are arranged as nodes are (see treeLayout.isMember()), by the relations they’re an end of (eg: isa): not by their own ends (their domain and range), unless the table says so. Nodes in a context window (or that are one) aren’t arranged.
