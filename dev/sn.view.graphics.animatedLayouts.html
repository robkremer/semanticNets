sn.view.graphics.animatedLayouts.leap(snap, kMinDist, tries=20)¶

A leap, like a mutation: an object out on the canvas is thrown somewhere else entirely – anywhere across the graph, or well out into the free space around it (up to LEAP_REACH of its size beyond its edges) – at least LEAP_MIN of the graph’s diagonal away. Its relations then pull it back in from wherever it lands, by another way: around walls of other objects that kept it from what it’s related to, which its springs, a step at a time, couldn’t pull it through. The object is picked at random: for LEAP_ANY of the leaps, from all those out on the canvas, evenly; for the rest, the objects with the longest links are the likeliest to go (by the square of their stretch()) – not those with the most links: moving a hub is more likely a fatal mutation. Each takes its leaves (what’s related to it alone) along, if they’re near it (see LEAP_LEAF): not, say, the one it’s stuck away from. (A window leaps with what it contains.) Plain arithmetic: safe in a thread.

Parameters:

  • snap (Snapshot) – The graph.

  • kMinDist (float) – The gap below which links don’t pull.

  • tries (int) – How many places to try, for one far enough away.

Returns:

(the object’s index and its leaves’, dx, dy), or None for no leap.

Return type:

tuple[list[int], float, float] | None

sn.view.graphics.animatedLayouts.forces(snap, e, gMaxDist, k, kMinDist, maxDisplacement, flow=None, flowStrength=0.0, flowSpacing=40.0, heat=0.0, arranging=False, layerGain=0.2, straighten=False, pull=30.0, leaps=None)¶

The displacement of each object in snap (in its order), with distances measured between the objects’ edges (see gap()), so big objects – context windows, long labels – are as solid as they look:

  • repulsion between every two objects less than gMaxDist apart, of e / gap (like charged particles: as between centres for small objects), along the line between their centres – overlapping objects push hardest;

  • attraction along links, stretched beyond kMinDist (like elastic bands), which pull until the objects’ edges nearly touch;

  • with a flow direction, a current: the objects of snap.directed’s relations are layered along it by rank (see rankOf()) – dsts upstream of their srcs, eg: for “isa” flowing down, superclasses above their subclasses: a class diagram – each pulled (by up to flowStrength) to its layer, flowSpacing apart (between edges). The layers are centred on where those objects are, and what’s left of any overall drift is taken out, so the current arranges things without carrying them away. (Across the current, repulsion and the relations’ pull arrange them as usual.);

each capped at maxDisplacement in x and in y.

What’s in a context window (see Snapshot.owners) is pushed and pulled only by what’s in the same window, and kept in its pane (see Snapshot.regions): anything outside it floats in (by up to pull, or a quarter of the way, in a step), and nothing inside it is moved out. Its move is on top of its window’s: it moves with the window, and within it.

The canvas’s top and left edges are kept by sliding the whole arrangement down or right exactly as needed, so no object’s footprint – its box and any text spilling out of it – goes off them, without stopping objects there one by one. Plain arithmetic: safe in a thread.

Parameters:

  • snap (Snapshot) – The graph.

  • e (float) – The repulsive strength of objects for one another.

  • gMaxDist (float) – The furthest apart (between edges) objects repel one another.

  • k (float) – The stiffness of the links.

  • kMinDist (float) – The gap below which links don’t pull.

  • maxDisplacement (float) – The most an object moves (in x and in y) in one step.

  • flow (tuple[float, float] | None) – The current’s direction, a unit vector (see FLOW_DIRECTIONS); None for none.

  • flowStrength (float) – The most the current pushes an object in a step.

  • flowSpacing (float) – The gap (between edges, along the current) it puts between a relation’s dst and src.

  • heat (float) – The most each object is jiggled at random (in x and in y) in a step: lets the layout out of dead ends (eg: a tree pointing straight against the current), as in simulated annealing.

  • arranging (bool) – While the current is first arranging things: objects it layers repel one another only across it, so they can slide past one another to their layers (eg: a tree turning around). (Objects in the same layer always repel one another only across it: they’re on one line.)

  • layerGain (float) – The fraction of the way to its layer the current pulls an object in a step (up to flowStrength).

  • straighten (bool) – Put the objects the current layers onto their layers’ lines: along the current, they move only toward their layers (other forces act on them only across it).

  • pull (float) – The least a contained object outside its pane is moved toward it in a step (when it’s further).

  • leaps (dict[int, tuple[float, float]] | None) – Objects (by index) that leap this step (see leap()), and how far: their moves, uncapped, instead of the forces’ (with what’s in them, as usual).

Returns:

(dx, dy) for each object: how far it moves on the canvas (with its window’s move).

Return type:

list[tuple[float, float]]

class sn.view.graphics.animatedLayouts.FieldsLayout(view, e=1000.0, gMaxDist=200.0, k=0.005, kMinDist=0.0, displacementSensitivity=5.0, maxDisplacement=10.0, dtime=100)¶

Bases: object

The animation layout – a “physics” layout: objects repel one another like similarly charged particles, and relations pull what they connect together like elastic bands – run as an animation, step by step, until nothing moves much (or it’s stopped). Context windows move as wholes, carrying their contents, which are laid out inside them, each in its pane (see Snapshot).

Each step’s forces are calculated from a Snapshot of the graph (see forces()); here, on the GUI thread, a step at a time between Tk events (see FieldsLayout_threaded for doing it in a thread). The whole run is one undoable command.

Use:

f = FieldsLayout(view, ...)
f()        # start (or keep going)
f.kill()   # stop

_settleLabels(view)¶

What a move does when it’s dropped, for every floating relation label: put it back between its ends, and if it lands on something, find it a free place nearby (see VRelation.reposition(): the local layout). During the run the labels just follow their ends – relations aren’t bodies in the layout – so they can end up on top of objects (eg: a node in a layer between the relation’s ends). Labels placed by hand (not floating) stay put, as they do after an ordinary move.

Parameters:

Return type:

None

_cool(energy)¶

Note a step’s total movement, energy: if it hasn’t fallen meaningfully (see progress) for patience steps, the layout isn’t converging – it’s jiggling about a balance point – so cool down (see temperature).

While a current flows (see flowDirection), there’s first an arranging phase, with no cooling at all, as long as the current’s relations keep coming into line (their misalignment() falls by at least alignProgress within alignPatience steps): turning a tree around takes a while, and starts slowly. Then it settles, as above.

Parameters:

energy (float) – The total of how far everything moved in the step.

_finish()¶

End the run: let the floating relation labels find free places (see _settleLabels()), and record all its moves as one undoable command.

class sn.view.graphics.animatedLayouts.FieldsLayout_threaded(view, e=1000.0, gMaxDist=200.0, k=0.005, kMinDist=0.0, displacementSensitivity=5.0, maxDisplacement=10.0, dtime=100)¶

Bases: FieldsLayout

A FieldsLayout whose forces are calculated in a thread, so the GUI stays responsive with large graphs: each step’s Snapshot is taken, and its moves made, on the GUI thread; only the arithmetic (forces()) is done in the thread – which touches no GUI objects (Tk isn’t thread-safe).

Parameters:

  • view (SNView)

  • e (float)

  • gMaxDist (float)

  • k (float)

  • kMinDist (float)

  • displacementSensitivity (float)

  • maxDisplacement (float)

  • dtime (int)

POLL_MS = 15¶

How often (ms) the GUI thread checks whether the thread’s step is done.

_call(autoOff)¶

Take a snapshot, and calculate its forces in a thread. The thread makes no Tk calls at all – it just leaves its result – and the GUI thread picks it up (see _poll()): the safe way with Tk, whatever thread Tk’s event loop runs in.

Parameters:

autoOff (bool) – As for FieldsLayout.__call__().

_poll(result, autoOff)¶

On the GUI thread: if the thread’s step is done, make its moves (see _commitChanges()); otherwise check again shortly.

Parameters:

  • result (SimpleQueue) – Where the thread leaves ((changes, misalignment, pins, leapt), error): see _work().

  • autoOff (bool) – As for FieldsLayout.__call__().
