sn.view.graphics.layout module¶

Layout heuristics: ways of arranging a view’s objects. There are three kinds (see CATEGORIES), and a heuristic may be more than one:

  • static (STATIC): chosen, and done all at once, as one undoable command (see LayoutHeuristic.arrange()): eg: TreeLayout (in treeLayout), “gather nodes”.

  • dynamic (DYNAMIC): the view’s current one (SNGView.localLayout: one at a time) keeps things arranged as they change: it places what’s made or dropped (see LayoutHeuristic.placeNode()), and after each command that changes the view, adjusts the rest (see LayoutHeuristic.adjust()), as part of the same undoable command: eg: FindFree (“avoid overlaps”: the default), TreeLayout, NoLayout.

  • animated (ANIMATED): runs in the background until things settle: eg: animatedLayouts.FieldsLayout.

Each heuristic declares the parameters its constructor takes (see LayoutParameter): a program can construct one with them directly (eg: TreeLayout(view, relation=r, direction="down").arrange()), and the view’s menus ask for them with a dialog made from them (see LayoutHeuristic.askParameters()). Heuristics are listed by name in LAYOUTS (see registerLayout()), which the view’s “layout” menu is made from.

Their moves are made by LayoutMoves: one undoable command, animated (tweened: see tween()) when it’s first done.

placeBatch(nodes, near)¶

As the view’s dynamic layout: put nodes – many, just made together (eg: by expanding near: see VObject.expand()), and not placed yet – all at once. Placed one by one (see placeNode()), each would be tried against everything placed before it, which for thousands takes minutes. By default, in rows, in their order: a block about BATCH_ASPECT times as wide as it’s tall, centred below near, as close below it as clears what’s there (but not nodes, nor their relations’ labels: they follow their ends).

sn.view.graphics.layout._moveAll(view, moves)¶

Move each object to (x, y) (its top left) at once – without redrawing its relations as it goes (see VObject.moveTo()’s fast) – and then redraw each relation of what moved, once (see VRelation.reposition(): a floating label goes between its ends, wherever they’ve gone). Moving them one by one redraws every relation of each as it moves – one between two moved objects twice – which is slow with a lot of them (eg: a module’s dozens of imports).

sn.view.graphics.layout.gatherAxisShifts(intervals, *, gapFactor=6.0, minGap=300.0)¶

One axis of gatherShifts(): how far to shift each interval so that no group of intervals is stranded far from the main pack.

The intervals are swept in order of their low ends, keeping track of the furthest high end so far; wherever the next interval starts more than maxGap beyond that, a new run begins. The run with the most intervals is the pack and stays put. Every oversized gap is shrunk to targetGap, and everything beyond it (away from the pack) shifts toward the pack by the amount removed – so a stranded group keeps its internal arrangement.

The thresholds come from the drawing itself: with S the median interval length and G the typical gap between consecutive runs of overlapping intervals, maxGap = max(gapFactor * max(G, S), minGap) and targetGap = max(G, S/2). G is the median of the smaller half of the gaps, so that it isn’t dominated by the very outliers it’s meant to find (in a small drawing, one or two huge gaps can be most of the gaps).

It’s O(n log n) (one sort) and uses no distances – just comparisons and differences.

Parameters:

  • intervals (Sequence[tuple[float, float]]) – (low, high) extents along one axis.

  • gapFactor (float) – How many times the typical spacing a gap must exceed to count as stranding what lies beyond it.

  • minGap (float) – No gap smaller than this counts as stranding, however tightly packed the rest of the drawing is – so a normal (if loose) layout is left alone.

Returns:

The shift for each interval, in the same order.

Return type:

list[float]

sn.view.graphics.layout.clearOf(box, keepOut, obstacles=[], *, within=None, gap=10.0)¶

How far to move box – as little as can be – so it’s gap clear of every rect in keepOut (eg: windows it doesn’t belong in), clear of obstacles too (by gap / 2), and inside within (if given: eg: its own pane). The candidates are the positions that just clear something (or line up with within’s edges), alone and in pairs (one across, one down), so the result is the nearest one there is, but not necessarily the very nearest point.
