sn.view.graphics.sngView module¶

The graphical view (SNGView): a window’s canvas of view objects (see sn.view.graphics.vobjects) showing the objects of one model (and of the models it builds on), saved as a .snv file. This module is the view as a database – making it, saving and reading it, observing its model, finding the view objects of a model object; what else it does is in its parts, the mixins it inherits (see viewBase):

  • viewViewport.ViewportMixin: zooming, scrolling, painting;

  • viewReasoning.ReasoningMixin: showing reasoning;

  • viewLayouts.LayoutsMixin: layouts, and the layout panel;

  • viewFiles.FilesMixin: files, window menus, locking;

  • viewVisibility.VisibilityMixin: hidden and erased categories;

  • viewContainment.ContainmentMixin: context windows;

  • viewEditing.EditingMixin: new objects, cut, copy and paste, erasing;

  • viewInput.InputMixin: the mouse, keys and menus.

Its commands are in sngContext.SNGContext, its undo list in viewCommands.ViewDoList.

BATCHED_UPDATE_BOXES = 50¶

A notification that takes out, or puts back, at least this many of a view’s boxes has their items hidden or shown in one pass over the canvas (see update(), batchedItemStates()). Measured: the pass takes as long as finding about 50 boxes’ items one by one on a canvas of 50,000 items, about 125 on one of 17,000, and more on smaller ones – where both are quick.

_changesManyBoxes(subject, topics)¶
Parameters:

  • topics (tuple[tuple[str, tuple[Any, ...]], ...]) – What it says.

Returns:

Whether at least BATCHED_UPDATE_BOXES of topics take one of this view’s boxes out (“del”), or put one back (“new”). Not just how many “del”s and “new”s there are: they may be of objects it doesn’t show (eg: reasoning cleared, or a super-model’s objects removed in another window), and then the pass over the canvas would be wasted.
