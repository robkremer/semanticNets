sn.view.graphics.treeLayout module¶

The tree layout (see TreeLayout): a view’s nodes arranged as trees – or a forest – by a relation type (and its subtypes): each node’s children in a column (or row) beside it, the next level out, each parent level with the middle of its children, and the relations’ labels lined up just before the children. Static (“arrange now”) and dynamic (“keep arranged”: it opens up for what’s added, and closes up over what’s removed or hidden).

treePositions() is the geometry, on its own (no view): where each node of some trees goes.

Created on Oct 10, 2026

Code author: Rob Kremer <robkremer@me.com>

sn.view.graphics.treeLayout.treePositions(roots, children, sizes, *, direction='right', spacing=20.0, levelGaps=None, levelGap=60.0, compact=False)¶

Lay out some trees, each on its own: the root at depth 0, each node’s children at the next depth – the depths in lines (columns, for “right”), each as deep as its deepest node, levelGaps apart – the leaves spacing apart across, each parent level with the middle of its children, and the groups of a node’s children spacing further apart.

Parameters:

  • roots (list[K]) – The trees’ roots.

  • children (dict[K, list[list[K]]]) – Each node’s children, in groups (eg: by the type of relation to them), in order. Each node is laid out once: under the first parent it’s met under (a node met again is left out there).

  • sizes (Mapping[K, tuple[float, float]]) – Each node’s (width, height).

  • direction (str) – Which way the trees grow: one of DIRECTIONS.

  • spacing (float) – The space between neighbouring nodes across a level.

  • levelGaps (dict[int, float] | None) – The space between depth n and depth n + 1, by n, if not levelGap.

  • levelGap (float) – The space between depths not in levelGaps.

  • compact (bool) – If False, each subtree is wholly after the one before it, across (its leaves after the last of the one before’s). If True, each is only as far along as the depths they both have need: a subtree tucks in beside the one before wherever that one’s deeper levels leave room (each depth’s extent across – its contour – is kept, much as in the Reingold-Tilford tree drawing). The depths are the same either way; and either way, nothing overlaps, and the relations from parents to children don’t cross.

Returns:

Where each node goes (its top left), with each tree’s root at (0, 0) – or, growing left or up, with its root’s far side at 0 – and each tree’s nodes, and their bounding box (x1, y1, x2, y2), by root.

Return type:

tuple[dict[K, tuple[float, float]], list[tuple[K, list[float]]]]

class sn.view.graphics.treeLayout.TreeLayout(view, relation=None, parentEnd=None, **kwargs)¶

Bases: LayoutHeuristic

Arrange nodes as trees by the relation types in a table (relations: see relationTable.RelationTable) whose rows shape the trees: each relation of one of those types (shown in the view) from a parent to a child – which end’s the parent its row says – puts the child one level out from the parent, the way the trees grow (direction). A node’s children (laid out one after another across, or – compact – tucked in beside each other where there’s room: see treePositions()) are grouped by the row they’re related by, and then by which of its relation type’s direct subtypes (in the order those were made: eg: for “includes”, a package’s modules, then its sub-packages), and each group’s in order. A node with more than one parent is put under the one related by the highest row (the first related, of those); its other relations are left floating, as are those that would make a cycle. Each tree relation’s label is put just before its child, on the straight line from the parent (so the labels line up, and the lines stay straight). The rows that pull things together (and those that shape the trees) are what join trees (see balloonLayout.TREES) and put the nodes in no tree near what they’re related to (others).

Relation types shown are arranged as nodes are (see isMember()): eg: by isa, has-parent under has-ancestor – and, by the table’s row for them (see relationTable.TERMINALS), by their own ends (their domain and range), as if each were related to them. Nodes in a context window (or that are one) aren’t arranged.

As a static layout (see arrange()): the children are in order of name; the trees are put one after another (across the way they grow) from the top left of what’s arranged; and the nodes in no tree are left where they are, put in rows after the trees, or put near what they’re related to (others: see OTHERS).

As a dynamic layout (see adjust()): after each command, the trees are laid out again, each from where its root is: children in the order they are already, and new ones after them, by name. So a tree opens up for what’s added to it, and closes up over what’s removed or hidden – and what’s after it, across (the trees and the nodes in no tree alongside it), moves along by as much (see _keepPlaces()). A tree node the user drags (and drops) takes its subtree with it: it’s pinned there, as the root of a tree of its own (room is made for its subtree), until the next static tree layout. The nodes in no tree are left where they are, put in rows after the trees, or put near what they’re related to (others), again each time – except those the user drops somewhere, which are pinned there too. Nothing’s left on another node: what would be is pushed further across.

  • parentEnd (str | None)

_keepPlaces(trees, children, rel, others, fresh, gap)¶

Dynamically: where the trees go – each from where its root is – and the nodes in no tree. When a tree grows or shrinks across (eg: something’s added to it, or taken out), what’s after it across, and alongside it (the trees and the nodes in no tree), moves along by as much: so things open up, and close up. (By the most, of the trees before it, if there’s more than one.) A tree just dropped (see fresh) is taken to have been just its root, there: room is made for the rest. Then anything that would be on (or within gap of) a node already placed – trees in order across, then the nodes in no tree – is pushed further across, past it; but a tree just dropped goes to the nearest clear place, either way. Node by node, not by the trees’ boxes, so a tree can be dropped in a gap in another.

Parameters:

  • trees (list[tuple[VObject, list[float]]]) – The trees, laid out on their own (see treePositions()), by root.

  • children (dict[VObject, list[list[VObject]]]) – As for treePositions().

  • rel (dict[VObject, tuple[float, float]]) – Where treePositions() put each node, in its own tree.

  • others (list[VObject]) – The nodes in no tree.

  • fresh (set[int]) – The ids of the trees’ roots just dropped.

  • gap (float) – The space kept between nodes of different trees (and those in no tree).

Returns:

Where they go (their top left): those that move.

Return type:

dict[VObject, tuple[float, float]]

adjust(command)¶

After a command: lay the trees out again (see compute()). A tree node the user dropped (see SNGView._Move) that has a parent is pinned where it’s dropped, as the root of a tree of its own (with its subtree); a node in no tree the user dropped is pinned there too, so it’s left there, not put in rows or near what it’s related to (see others).

Parameters:

command (Command)

Return type:

LayoutMoves | None

pinnable(drops)¶
Parameters:

drops (list[VObject]) – Nodes the user dropped.

Returns:

The ids of those to pin: all but the roots of trees (a root’s kept where it is anyway). By the trees as structure() finds them, not just by the relations there are: a node that’s only the parent end of relations whose children all went under other parents (eg: a second base class) is in no tree, and is put near what it’s related to (see others) unless it’s pinned – so, dropped, it’d go straight back.

set[int]
